On Sunday night, Mom packed her suitcase. She was going to Chicago for work. "I am going to be away for one week," she said. "Seven days. Can you three be okay without me?"

"Of course," said Dad. He stood up and put his hands on his hips. "While you are away, I am going to cook dinner every night. Real dinner. Not pizza. I am going to be a chef!"

My sister Mia and I looked at each other. Dad can make toast. Dad can make coffee. Once, he made a sandwich with peanut butter and pickles. That was the end of his cooking.

Mom looked at Dad for a long time. "Okay," she said slowly. "The recipes are in the blue book in the kitchen. The fire extinguisher is under the sink."

"Ha ha," said Dad. "Very funny."

On Monday, Dad came home early. He was very excited. He had three bags of food. "Tonight," he said, "we are going to have spaghetti with tomato sauce. It is going to be the best spaghetti in the world."

First, he put on Mom's apron. It was pink with little yellow flowers. Then he opened the blue recipe book. Next, he filled the biggest pot with water. After that, he looked at the recipe for a long time.

"It says one teaspoon of salt," he said. "But I am a chef. Chefs do not measure. Chefs feel."

He put in a lot of salt. Then he felt something else. He put in some blue food coloring. "For fun," he said.

Finally, dinner was ready. The spaghetti was blue. Bright blue. The sauce was red. Together, they looked like a sad clown.

"It is a new style," said Dad. "Everybody is going to love it."

Mia tasted it. I tasted it. It was very, very salty. It tasted like the ocean. Mia drank three glasses of water.

"Dad," said Mia, "are you going to cook spaghetti again?"

"No," said Dad. "Tomorrow I am going to make something different."

On Tuesday, Dad made soup. He put in every vegetable in the fridge. Carrots, potatoes, onions, broccoli, and one very old cucumber. He also put in some leftover pancakes. "Nothing goes to waste in my kitchen," he said. The soup was brown and thick. It was so thick that the spoon stood up in the middle of the bowl. Mia called it "soup cement."

On Wednesday, Dad made fish. He put the fish in the oven. Then he watched a soccer game on TV. He forgot the fish. After one hour, the smoke alarm started to scream. BEEP! BEEP! BEEP! The kitchen was full of gray smoke. Dad ran into the kitchen. He opened all the windows. He waved a towel at the alarm. Our dog, Biscuit, ran around and barked at the smoke.

The fish was black. It was as hard as a rock. Dad looked at it. "I think," he said, "this fish is extra crispy."

"Dad," I said, "I will call the pizza shop."

"No!" said Dad. "No pizza! I made a promise. I am a chef!"

We ate cereal for dinner. Dad ate cereal too. He looked tired.

On Thursday, Mom called us on video. "How is everything?" she asked. "How is the cooking?"

Dad smiled a big smile. "Wonderful," he said. "Every dinner is an adventure."

Behind him, Mia held up a piece of paper. It said, "HELP." Mom laughed so hard that she had to put the phone down.

On Friday, Dad decided to make dessert. "Everybody likes dessert," he said. "Tonight we are going to have popcorn and chocolate cake."

The popcorn came first. Dad did not use the small popcorn bags. He used a big bag of popcorn seeds and a big pot. He put in one cup of seeds. Then he thought, "More is better." He put in the whole bag. He put the lid on the pot. He turned the fire to high.

"Dad," I said, "I think that is too much."

"Trust the chef," said Dad.

For one minute, nothing happened. Then we heard it. Pop. Pop, pop. Pop, pop, pop, pop! POP-POP-POP-POP-POP!

The lid started to move. It went up, and it came down. It went up higher. Popcorn came out from under it.

"You must not open the pot!" Dad shouted. "Hold the lid!"

Mia held the lid with a big spoon. I held it with a towel. Dad held it with his hands. The popcorn did not care. BANG! The lid flew up to the ceiling. Popcorn exploded all over the kitchen like white snow. It was on the floor. It was on the table. It was in the sink. It was on top of the fridge. Some of it was in Dad's hair.

Biscuit was the happiest dog in the world. He ran around and ate popcorn from the floor. He ate so much that he lay down on his back and did not move.

We stood in the kitchen. We were covered in popcorn. Then Mia started to laugh. I started to laugh. Finally, Dad laughed too. We laughed until our stomachs hurt.

"Okay," said Dad. "No chocolate cake tonight. We have to clean the kitchen."

On Saturday, Dad did something different. He did not cook alone. He said, "Today, we are going to cook together. And we are going to follow the recipe. Exactly."

He gave Mia the measuring cups. He gave me the eggs. He read the recipe out loud, slowly. "One cup of flour. Two eggs. Half a cup of sugar." We did everything carefully. We made a simple chicken with rice and green beans. We made a small chocolate cake. Nothing was blue. Nothing exploded. Nothing went black.

It was delicious. Really delicious. Dad was so proud. He took a picture of his plate and sent it to Mom.

On Sunday afternoon, Mom came home. She opened the front door. She smelled the air. "Something smells good," she said. She walked into the kitchen. The table was ready. There was chicken, rice, and the second chocolate cake of the week.

"Who made this?" she asked.

"The chefs," said Dad. He pointed at Mia and me. "All three of us."

Mom tasted the chicken. "This is very good," she said. Then she looked up. She saw something on top of the kitchen cupboard. It was a little piece of popcorn. She looked at Dad. "What is that?"

Dad smiled. "That," he said, "is a long story. And I am going to tell you everything. But first, have some cake."
