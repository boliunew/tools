Everybody wants to be healthy. We all know that we should eat vegetables, sleep more, and exercise regularly. But knowing something and doing it are two very different things. Most people have tried to start a healthy habit at least once. Maybe they decided to go running every morning, or to stop eating junk food. Usually, they lasted about a week. Then life got busy, and the old habits came back.

The good news is that healthy habits do not have to be difficult. You do not need to run a marathon or eat only salad. Small changes, if you do them every day, can make a big difference. In this article, we will look at six healthy habits that actually work, and we will explain why they are important. We will also give you some simple ideas to help you start.

Habit 1: Get enough sleep

Getting enough sleep is the first healthy habit, and maybe the most important one. Sleep is not a waste of time. While you are sleeping, your body is very busy. It repairs your muscles. It makes your immune system stronger, so you can fight colds and flu. And your brain organizes everything you learned during the day. Scientists have found that students who sleep well remember more information and get better grades.

How much sleep do you need? It depends on your age. Children between six and twelve need nine to twelve hours a night. Teenagers need eight to ten hours. Adults need seven to nine hours. But many people do not get enough. In fact, studies show that most teenagers sleep less than eight hours on school nights.

What happens when you do not sleep enough? If you sleep less than seven hours, you will feel tired and hungry the next day. You will find it hard to concentrate in class or at work. You will be in a worse mood, and you might get angry more easily. You will probably eat more junk food too, because a tired brain wants quick energy from sugar.

Here are some tips for better sleep. Go to bed and get up at the same time every day, even on weekends. Keep your bedroom dark, cool, and quiet. Avoid coffee, tea, and energy drinks in the afternoon and evening. And, most importantly, you shouldn't look at your phone for an hour before bed. The bright light from screens tells your brain that it is still daytime, so it is harder to fall asleep. Instead, read a book, take a warm shower, or listen to quiet music.

Habit 2: Eat a balanced diet

The word "diet" does not only mean eating less to lose weight. Your diet is simply all the food you eat. A balanced diet means you eat many different kinds of food in the right amounts.

A healthy plate has four main parts. Half of your plate should be vegetables and fruit. They give you vitamins, minerals, and fiber. Fiber helps your stomach work well. One quarter of your plate should be protein, like chicken, fish, eggs, beans, or tofu. Protein helps you build strong muscles. The last quarter should be whole grains, like brown rice, whole wheat bread, or oats. They give you long-lasting energy. And you need a small amount of healthy fats, from things like nuts, olive oil, and avocados.

The biggest problem in modern diets is sugar. Sugar is hidden in many foods that do not even taste sweet, like bread, ketchup, and pasta sauce. Many teenagers eat too much sugar and too many snacks. A single can of soda has about ten teaspoons of sugar. That is more than the recommended amount for a whole day. When you eat a lot of sugar, your energy goes up very fast, and then it crashes down. You feel tired, hungry, and grumpy.

You do not need to stop eating sweets completely. That is not realistic, and it is not fun. But try to make small changes. Eat fruit instead of candy for a snack. Choose plain yogurt and add your own fruit. Read the labels on food packages. If sugar is one of the first three ingredients, that food has a lot of it.

Another important tip: don't skip breakfast. People who eat breakfast usually have more energy in the morning and eat less junk food later in the day. A good breakfast does not need to be big. Eggs and toast, oatmeal with fruit, or yogurt with nuts are all great choices.

Habit 3: Move your body every day

Exercise is like medicine, but you do not need a doctor to get it. Regular exercise makes your heart and lungs stronger. It builds muscles and bones. It helps you keep a healthy weight. It also improves your mood. When you exercise, your brain releases chemicals called endorphins. Endorphins make you feel happy and relaxed. That is why many people say they feel great after a run or a game of basketball.

Experts say that teenagers should get at least sixty minutes of physical activity every day. Adults need at least thirty minutes on most days. This does not mean you have to go to the gym. Any activity that makes your heart beat faster counts. Walking, biking, dancing, swimming, playing soccer, and even cleaning the house are all exercise.

The secret is to find an activity that you enjoy. If you hate running, do not run. Try something else. Maybe you will love dancing, or rock climbing, or skateboarding. If you enjoy your exercise, you will keep doing it. It also helps to exercise with a friend. You can encourage each other, and it is more fun.

Here are some easy ways to move more every day. Take the stairs instead of the elevator. Walk or bike to school if it is safe. Get off the bus one stop early and walk the rest of the way. Stand up and stretch every hour when you are studying. Play outside with your friends or your dog instead of watching TV.

Habit 4: Drink more water

About sixty percent of your body is water. You lose water all day long when you breathe, sweat, and go to the bathroom. If you do not drink enough, you can become dehydrated. Dehydration can cause headaches, tiredness, and difficulty concentrating. Many people think they are hungry when they are actually thirsty.

How much water do you need? A simple rule is to drink about six to eight glasses a day, and more if it is hot or if you exercise. Water is better than juice or soda, because it has no sugar and no calories. If you do not like plain water, add a slice of lemon, some mint leaves, or a few pieces of fruit.

A great habit is to carry a water bottle with you everywhere. Fill it in the morning, and try to finish it by lunch. Then fill it again. When you see the bottle, you will remember to drink.

Habit 5: Control your screen time

Phones, tablets, computers, and TVs are part of our lives. They are useful for school, work, and staying in touch with friends. But too much screen time can cause problems. It can hurt your eyes and your neck. It can make you sit still for too many hours. It can make it harder to sleep. And spending a lot of time on social media can make some people feel lonely, anxious, or unhappy with their lives.

You do not have to throw away your phone. Instead, try to use it more carefully. Here are some ideas. Check how many hours you spend on your phone each day. Most phones can show you this information. You might be surprised. Turn off notifications for apps that are not important. Keep your phone out of your bedroom at night. Have "no phone" times, for example during meals with your family. And when you have free time, try to do something that is not on a screen at least once a day: read a book, draw, cook, play an instrument, or go outside.

There is also a simple rule for your eyes, called the 20-20-20 rule. Every twenty minutes, look at something twenty feet away for twenty seconds. This gives your eye muscles a short rest.

Habit 6: Manage stress

Everybody feels stressed sometimes. Students feel stressed about tests, homework, and friendships. Adults feel stressed about work, money, and family. A little stress is normal, and it can even help you. It can give you energy to finish a project or to do well in a game. But too much stress for a long time is bad for your health. It can cause headaches, stomachaches, sleep problems, and sadness.

The good news is that there are many ways to manage stress. Exercise is one of the best, as we saw above. Sleeping well also helps a lot. Here are some other strategies. Talk to someone you trust, like a friend, a parent, or a teacher. Just talking about your problem can make you feel better. Write your thoughts in a journal. Try deep breathing: breathe in slowly for four seconds, hold for four seconds, and breathe out for four seconds. Do this five times. Spend time in nature, even if it is just a park. Do something creative, like drawing or playing music. And remember to laugh. Watch a funny video or spend time with funny friends. Laughing really is good medicine.

Another important skill is planning. A lot of stress comes from having too many things to do at the same time. Use a planner or a calendar. Write down your homework, your tests, and your activities. Break big tasks into small steps. For example, if you have a big project, do a little bit every day instead of doing everything the night before. You will feel much calmer.

How to build a new habit

Now you know six healthy habits. But how can you actually make them part of your life? Scientists who study habits have discovered some useful tricks.

First, start small. Do not try to change everything at once. Choose one habit, and make it very easy. For example, if you want to exercise more, start with a ten-minute walk after dinner. If you want to drink more water, start by drinking one glass when you wake up. Small successes give you confidence.

Second, connect the new habit to something you already do. For example: "After I brush my teeth in the morning, I will drink a glass of water." Or: "After I get home from school, I will put on my shoes and walk around the block." Your old habit becomes a reminder for your new habit.

Third, make it easy to do the right thing and hard to do the wrong thing. If you want to eat more fruit, put a bowl of fruit on the kitchen table. If you want to eat fewer chips, do not keep chips in the house. If you want to go running in the morning, put your running shoes next to your bed.

Fourth, track your progress. Use a calendar and draw an X on every day that you do your new habit. After a few days, you will have a chain of Xs. You will not want to break the chain.

Finally, be patient and kind to yourself. Studies show that it takes about two months, on average, for a new behavior to become automatic. You will make mistakes. You will miss some days. That is normal. If you miss a day, do not give up. Just start again the next day. Remember, the goal is not to be perfect. The goal is to be a little bit healthier than yesterday.

Your healthy habits checklist

Here is a simple daily checklist. How many can you do today?

I slept at least eight hours last night.
I ate breakfast this morning.
I ate at least five portions of fruit and vegetables today.
I drank six to eight glasses of water.
I moved my body for at least sixty minutes.
I put my phone away one hour before bed.
I did something to relax or have fun.

Do not worry if you cannot check every box today. Choose one, and start there. Your future self will thank you.
