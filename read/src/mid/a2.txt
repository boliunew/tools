Friends are one of the most important parts of our lives. They make us laugh when we are sad. They help us when we have problems. They share our happy moments and our difficult moments. Scientists have even found that people who have good friends live longer, healthier, and happier lives. But making friends is not always easy, and keeping friends can be even harder. In this article, we will talk about how to make new friends, how to be a good friend, and how to get along with people, even when you do not agree with them.

Why making friends can be hard

Many people find it difficult to make new friends. Maybe you have moved to a new city. Maybe you have started at a new school or a new job. Maybe you are shy, and you feel nervous when you talk to people you do not know. These feelings are completely normal. In fact, most people feel a little nervous when they meet strangers. The difference is that some people have learned how to manage those feelings.

The good news is that making friends is a skill. Like any skill, you can learn it and get better at it with practice. You do not have to be the funniest person in the room, or the most popular. You just have to be friendly, interested in other people, and patient.

Step 1: Be open and friendly

The first step is to show people that you are open to new friendships. One of the easiest ways is to smile. A smile tells people that you are friendly and that it is safe to talk to you. My mom always told me to smile and say hello first. When I was younger, I thought that was silly advice. Now I know she was right. Most people are waiting for someone else to say hello first. If you do it, they will usually be happy and relieved.

Body language is also important. If you stand with your arms crossed and look at your phone, people will think you do not want to talk. Instead, keep your phone in your pocket, look up, and make eye contact. Not too much eye contact, of course. Staring at people is strange. Just look at them naturally when they are talking.

Step 2: Start a conversation

Starting a conversation is the part that scares most people. What should you say? The secret is that you do not need to say anything clever. Simple is best. Here are some easy ways to start a conversation:

Comment on something around you. "This line is so long today!" "Wow, it's really hot in here."

Ask a question. "Do you know what time the class starts?" "Is this seat free?" "How do you know the host?"

Give a compliment. "I like your shoes. Where did you get them?" "That was a great question you asked in class."

Introduce yourself. "Hi, I'm Alex. I'm new here."

After the first sentence, keep the conversation going with questions. People love talking about themselves, so ask about their interests, their weekend, or their opinions. You could ask them about their weekend, their favorite music, or a TV show they are watching. Use open questions, which are questions that need more than a "yes" or "no" answer. For example, instead of "Did you like the movie?", ask "What did you think of the movie?" Then, listen carefully to the answer, and ask another question about what they said.

Step 3: Find things in common

Friendships usually grow from shared interests. If you both love basketball, video games, cooking, or the same band, you will always have something to talk about. That is why one of the best ways to make friends is to join a club, a team, or a class. You will meet people who like the same things as you, and you will see them regularly. Seeing someone again and again is very important. Research shows that people usually become friends after spending many hours together.

So, if you want to make friends, say yes to invitations. Go to the party, even if you feel a little nervous. Join the after-school club. Volunteer in your community. Sign up for a sports team or a music group. Each activity is a new chance to meet people.

Step 4: Be yourself

Some people try to change themselves to make others like them. They pretend to like things they do not like, or they act differently with different groups. This might work for a short time, but it does not create real friendships. Real friends like you for who you are. So be yourself. Talk about the things you really care about. Laugh at the things you really find funny. The right people will like the real you.

What makes a good friend?

Making a new friend is only the beginning. To keep a friendship strong, you need to be a good friend yourself. What does that mean? We asked a group of teenagers, and here are their most common answers.

A good friend is someone who listens to you. When you have a problem, a good friend does not just wait for their turn to talk. They really listen, and they try to understand how you feel.

A good friend is honest. They tell you the truth, even when it is difficult. If you have spinach in your teeth, they tell you. If you are making a bad decision, they tell you, kindly.

A good friend is loyal. They do not talk badly about you behind your back. They keep your secrets. They stand up for you when other people are unkind.

A good friend supports you. They celebrate your successes, and they help you when you fail. They come to your soccer game, your concert, or your birthday party. They send you a message when they know you are having a bad day.

A good friend respects you. They respect your opinions, even when they disagree. They respect your time, your things, and your feelings.

My best friend, Maya, is all of these things. We have been best friends since second grade. That is almost eight years. We have had many arguments, but we have always solved them. She knows me better than anyone else, except maybe my mom.

When friends disagree

Every friendship has problems sometimes. Friends argue. They hurt each other's feelings. They make mistakes. This is normal. Disagreement does not mean the friendship is bad. What matters is how you handle it.

First, try to stay calm. When we are angry, we often say things that we do not really mean. If you feel very angry, take a break. Go for a walk, breathe deeply, and talk later when you feel calmer.

Second, talk about the problem directly. Do not ignore it, and do not complain about your friend to other people. Talk to your friend privately. Use "I" sentences instead of "you" sentences. For example, instead of saying "You always ignore my messages!", say "I felt sad when you didn't answer my messages yesterday." "You" sentences sound like an attack, and the other person will want to defend themselves. "I" sentences explain your feelings, and they make it easier for the other person to listen.

Third, listen to the other person's side. Very often, an argument is really a misunderstanding. Maybe your friend did not answer your messages because their phone was broken, or because they were having a family problem. You will not know until you ask.

Fourth, look for a solution together. Ask, "What can we do so this doesn't happen again?" Maybe you can agree to tell each other when you are busy. Maybe you need to spend more time together. A good solution works for both people.

How to apologize

Sometimes, you will be the one who made a mistake. When that happens, you need to apologize. A good apology is not just saying "sorry." A real apology has three parts.

First, say what you did wrong. "I'm sorry that I told other people your secret."

Second, show that you understand how the other person feels. "I know that made you feel embarrassed and hurt. You trusted me, and I broke your trust."

Third, say what you will do differently. "I won't do that again. From now on, your secrets will stay with me."

Do not add excuses. "I'm sorry, but you were annoying me" is not an apology. Also, do not expect the other person to forgive you immediately. Sometimes people need time. Be patient.

And when someone apologizes to you, try to forgive them. Forgiving does not mean that what they did was okay. It means that you choose to let go of your anger so that the friendship can continue. Holding on to anger for a long time hurts you more than anyone else.

Getting along with everyone

Not everyone in your life will be your friend, and that is fine. But you still need to get along with many different people: classmates, teammates, coworkers, neighbors, and family members. Here are some tips for getting along with people, even people you do not like very much.

Be polite. Say please, thank you, and excuse me. Greet people when you see them. Small manners make a big difference.

Respect differences. People have different backgrounds, beliefs, and opinions. You do not have to agree with everyone, but you should treat everyone with respect. You can say, "I see it differently, but I understand your point."

Do not gossip. Talking about people behind their back can feel fun for a moment, but it destroys trust. If someone gossips to you about other people, they will probably gossip about you too.

Help others. Offer to help a classmate with homework, carry something for a neighbor, or share your snacks. People remember kindness.

Keep your promises. If you say you will do something, do it. If you cannot, tell the person early. Being reliable is one of the most respected qualities a person can have.

Family relationships

We often forget that family members need friendship too. We sometimes treat our family worse than we treat strangers, because we know they will always be there. But family relationships need care, just like friendships.

Spend real time with your family. Eat meals together without phones. Ask your parents about their day. Play a game with your little brother or sister. Tell your grandparents that you love them. Many of these tips also work with family: listen, be honest, apologize when you are wrong, and forgive.

Feeling lonely

Sometimes, even when we try our best, we feel lonely. Maybe you have just moved, or maybe your old friends have changed. Loneliness is a very common feeling. Many people feel lonely, even people who seem popular.

If you feel lonely, remember that it is temporary. Keep trying to meet new people. Reach out to an old friend that you have not talked to for a long time. Send a simple message: "Hey, I was thinking about you. How are you?" Most people are happy to hear from an old friend. And if loneliness makes you feel very sad for a long time, talk to an adult you trust, like a parent, a teacher, or a school counselor.

Taking the next step

Sometimes you meet someone you like, you have a few good conversations, and then nothing happens. You see them in class or at work, you say hello, but you never become real friends. Often, the problem is that nobody takes the next step. So be brave and invite them to do something. Keep it simple and low-pressure: "A few of us are going to get pizza after the game. Do you want to come?" or "I'm going to the library on Saturday to study. Want to join?" If they say no, do not take it personally. People are busy. Try again another time. Each time you do this, you will feel a little more confident.

Final thoughts

Friendship is one of the best things in life, and it is worth the effort. Be brave and say hello first. Be curious about other people. Be honest, loyal, and kind. Apologize when you make mistakes, and forgive others when they make mistakes. And, most of all, be yourself. Somewhere out there, there is someone who is waiting to meet exactly the kind of person you are.
